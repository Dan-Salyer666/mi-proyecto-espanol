Tener estructural

❖
El verbo que sostiene un resultado

La página principal trata tener + sustantivo: un verbo y un nombre que forman un estado del sujeto. Esta trata el otro uso estructural — tener + objeto + predicado, donde lo que se describe no es el sujeto sino la condición en que se mantiene una cosa. El predicado puede ser un participio (tengo escritas las cartas), un adjetivo (me tienes harto) o una frase preposicional (lo tengo por buen médico).

①
El marcotener + objeto + predicado
The frame
tener + objeto directo + participio / adjetivo

A diferencia de tener + sustantivo, aquí tener no forma un estado del sujeto sino que atribuye una condición a un objeto. El objeto va primero; el predicado lo describe.

Tengo la cena preparada.
I have dinner ready.
Tenemos el auto arreglado.
We've got the car fixed.
Me tienes harto.
You've got me fed up.
Nos tiene preocupados.
He has us worried.
Tiene a los chicos contentos.
She's got the kids happy.
NOTA*

English has one "have + participle" and Spanish has two, doing completely different jobs.

Haber + participle is the perfect tense

He escrito tres cartas. Here haber is a pure auxiliary — no meaning of its own is left, the participle never changes form, and nothing can be inserted between the two words.

Tener + object + participle is not a tense at all

Tengo escritas tres cartas. Here tener keeps its lexical meaning — you genuinely hold or possess something — and the participle behaves like an adjective describing the object. Which is why it agrees in gender and number, and why the word order is flexible.

The test

Can you swap the participle for an adjective and keep the structure? Tengo la cena preparada / tengo la cena lista — both work, same shape. Try that with he preparado la cena and it collapses. That is the clearest evidence that tener here is a main verb with a predicate, not an auxiliary.

The resultative

El participio concuerda con el objeto en género y número. Esta es la diferencia visible más inmediata frente a haber, donde el participio no cambia nunca.

escribir · lavar · leer · preparar · terminar · guardar · ordenar · revisar · resolver tengo ido · tengo sido · tengo tenido · tengo sabido

Solo funciona con verbos transitivos que dejan un resultado visible en el objeto. Un verbo de movimiento o de estado no deja nada que sostener.

El matiz de acumulación
Tengo leídos tres capítulos.
I've got three chapters read. — progress toward a total.
Tengo revisada la mitad de las pruebas.
I've got half the tests marked.
Tengo vistos casi todos sus cuadros.
I've seen nearly all his paintings.
NOTA*

He escrito las cartas and tengo escritas las cartas describe the same event. The difference is where the sentence puts its weight.

Haber reports the action · tener reports the state

With haber, the letters got written and the sentence is about the writing. With tener, the letters are now in a written condition and the subject is the one holding them that way. That is why the construction feels like taking stock — it is the natural verb for inventories, progress checks, and "where do things stand" contexts.

Two consequences

  • It implies the result persists. Tengo escritas las cartas means they are still written and available. If they were destroyed the sentence stops being true — which is not the case for he escrito las cartas.
  • It wants a determined or quantified object: tengo escritas tres cartas, tengo escrita la correspondencia. A bare mass noun clashes with the sense of a bounded, completed task — tengo escrita correspondencia is rejected as unnatural.

There is often an accumulative reading too: tengo leídos tres capítulos suggests three so far, with more to come. That flavour is stronger still with llevar — see the next section.

③
Haber · tener · llevartres verbos, un participio
Three auxiliaries compared

Tres verbos, el mismo participio, tres significados distintos. Este contraste es la manera más rápida de fijar qué aporta cada uno.

VerboEjemploQué aporta
haberHe leído tres capítulos.La acción, sin más. Participio invariable.
tenerTengo leídos tres capítulos.El resultado, en mi poder. Concuerda.
llevarLlevo leídos tres capítulos.El avance dentro de un total. Concuerda.
He escrito cincuenta páginas.
I've written fifty pages.
Tengo escritas cincuenta páginas.
I've got fifty pages written.
Llevo escritas cincuenta páginas.
I'm fifty pages in.
¿Cuántas llevas revisadas?
How many have you got through?
NOTA*

Tener and llevar are close enough that the difference is worth naming precisely.

Tener takes stock

It describes current holdings, with no necessary implication about what comes next. Tengo revisadas veinte pruebas — twenty are done. Whether there are more is not part of the sentence.

Llevar measures progress

It presupposes a total and locates you inside it. Llevo revisadas veinte pruebas strongly implies a stack still to go. Ask ¿cuántas llevas? and you are asking how far through something someone is.

So llevar is the verb of the marathon and tener the verb of the inventory. They overlap constantly, and in many sentences either is fine — but where a total is in view, llevar is the more idiomatic choice.

④
Las formas fijassin concordancia
Frozen forms

Algunas combinaciones de tener + participio se han lexicalizado. Ya no llevan objeto nominal sino una cláusula o un infinitivo, y por eso el participio queda invariable.

Tengo entendido que renunció.
I understand he resigned.
Tengo oído que van a cerrar.
I hear they're going to close.
Tengo comprobado que así no funciona.
I know for a fact it doesn't work that way.
Tengo pensado mudarme.
I'm planning to move.
Tengo previsto llegar el jueves.
I'm scheduled to arrive Thursday.
El contraste
Con objeto — concuerdaCon cláusula — invariable
Tengo entendidas las instrucciones.Tengo entendido que renunció.
Tengo pensadas dos opciones.Tengo pensado mudarme.
NOTA*

The rule has not changed here; there is simply nothing for the participle to agree with.

Agreement in section ② happens because the participle describes a noun: las cartas is feminine plural, so escritas is feminine plural. In tengo entendido que renunció, what follows is a clause, not a noun. Clauses have no gender or number, so the participle falls back to the default masculine singular and stays there.

The same explanation covers tengo pensado mudarme — an infinitive, not a noun. And it predicts the contrast correctly: give the same phrase a real nominal object and agreement comes straight back. Tengo pensadas dos opciones.

What tengo comprobado que adds

This one is worth having actively. It is not a neutral "I checked" — it asserts a firm conclusion reached through repeated experience or rigorous verification, closer to I know for a fact or experience has taught me. Current and frequent on both sides of the Atlantic.

A warning that belongs here

Tengo entendido takes a bare que. Tengo entendido de que is dequeísmo — see section ⑤b of the parent page.

Note also that tener pensado and tener previsto appear on the parent page among the modals. That is the same construction seen from the other side: there as expressions of intent, here as frozen participles.

⑤
El reprochete lo tengo dicho
The standing reproach

Con verbos de habla, tener + participio adquiere un valor de insistencia: no es que lo haya dicho una vez, es que lo vengo diciendo.

Te lo tengo dicho: no toques eso.
I've told you: don't touch that.
Te tengo dicho que llames antes de venir.
I've told you to call before coming.
Ya te lo tengo advertido.
I've warned you about this already.
Nos lo tienen prohibido.
We're not allowed to.
NOTA*

A small, high-frequency corner of the construction, where the difference from the plain perfect is entirely pragmatic.

Te he dicho que no reports that the telling happened. Te lo tengo dicho reports that the telling is a standing fact — said before, said more than once, and still in force. English gets close with "I've told you" said with emphasis, or "I keep telling you."

A warning about tone

It is almost always reproachful. This is what a parent says to a child, or one adult says to another when patience is running out. It is not neutral, and using it at work or with someone you do not know well will land as scolding. Recognize it freely; deploy it deliberately.

The agreement rule from section ② still applies underneath: in te lo tengo dicho the object is the neuter lo, so the participle is masculine singular. Give it a plural nominal object and it agrees normally — te tengo dichas muchas cosas.

⑥
La negativatener + objeto + sin + infinitivo
The unfinished mirror

El resultativo tiene un contrario exacto. Para decir que algo sigue pendiente, se usa sin más infinitivo — y aquí no hay participio, así que no hay concordancia.

tener + objeto directo + sin + infinitivo
Tengo los platos sin lavar.
I've still got the dishes unwashed.
Tengo tres libros sin leer.
I've got three books still unread.
Todavía tenemos ese problema sin resolver.
We've still got that problem unresolved.
Tengo la mitad del informe sin escribir.
I've got half the report still unwritten.
El par
HechoPendiente
Tengo lavados los platos.Tengo los platos sin lavar.
Tengo leídos tres libros.Tengo tres libros sin leer.
Tengo resuelto el problema.Tengo el problema sin resolver.
NOTA*

This construction is much less taught than the affirmative resultative and just as common in speech.

The logic is transparent once you see the pair: tengo lavados los platos holds the dishes in a washed condition; tengo los platos sin lavar holds them in an unwashed one. Both are statements about the current state of your responsibilities. The second is the natural way to describe a backlog.

The form is simpler than the affirmative

Sin plus a bare infinitive, invariable, no agreement to worry about. Note the asymmetry — the affirmative uses a participle, the negative uses an infinitive. There is no tengo no lavados los platos.

Word order, and this one does matter

Unlike the affirmative resultative, the order here is not freely reversible. Tengo tres libros sin leer — object immediately after tener — is the standard order and dominates everywhere. The inversion tengo sin leer tres libros is grammatical but marked: it fronts the pending state for emphasis and reads as deliberately stylized. Default to object-first.

⑦
La valoracióntener a alguien por algo
To regard as

Con la preposición por, la construcción deja de describir un estado físico y pasa a expresar un juicio: cómo considero a alguien o algo.

tener + a alguien + por + sustantivo / adjetivo
Lo tengo por un buen médico.
I consider him a good doctor.
La tengo por muy capaz.
I regard her as very capable.
Siempre lo tuve por honesto.
I always took him to be honest.
Se tiene por experto en la materia.
He considers himself an expert on the subject.
NOTA*

This is the most formal item on the page, and it is worth knowing where it sits.

Tener a alguien por algo means to consider, to regard as — it reports the subject's assessment, not a fact. It belongs to careful speech and writing: a report, a character reference, a considered opinion. In casual conversation creo que es un buen médico or me parece muy capaz will be far more common.

The reflexive is where it turns up most

Se tiene por experto — he considers himself an expert — and this reading is frequently ironic, implying the self-assessment is generous. That flavour is worth hearing.

Two neighbours, recognition only

Tener en mucho and tener en poco literario — to hold someone in high or low regard. You will meet these in literature, essays and formal journalism, but modern speakers reach for tener en mucha estima or simply valorar mucho / poco. Passive vocabulary.
