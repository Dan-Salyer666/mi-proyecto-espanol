## The thesis

The parent page is about *tener + sustantivo* — a verb plus a noun making a state. This page is about the **other** structural use: *tener* plus an object plus **something predicated of that object**.

```
tener + objeto directo + predicado
```

The predicate can be a participle (*tengo escritas las cartas*), an adjective (*me tienes harto*), or a prepositional phrase (*lo tengo por buen médico*). In every case *tener* is not an auxiliary — it keeps its lexical weight, and what it means is roughly **"I hold X in this condition."**

That single frame unifies everything below, and it's why this belongs on its own page rather than as a section of the parent.

---

# ① El marco: *tener* + objeto + predicado

### Prosa

> A diferencia de *tener + sustantivo*, aquí *tener* no forma un estado del sujeto sino que **atribuye una condición a un objeto**. El objeto va primero; el predicado lo describe.

### Fórmula

```
tener + objeto directo + participio / adjetivo
```

### Ejemplos

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo **la cena preparada**. | I have dinner ready. |
| Tenemos **el auto arreglado**. | We've got the car fixed. |
| Me tienes **harto**. | You've got me fed up. |
| Nos tiene **preocupados**. | He has us worried. |
| Tiene **a los chicos contentos**. | She's got the kids happy. |
| Lo tengo **por un buen médico**. | I consider him a good doctor. |

### NOTA-A · ① — Why this is not the perfect tense

> **NOTA\*** English has one "have + participle" and Spanish has two, which do completely different jobs.
>
> **_Haber_ + participle is the perfect tense.** *He escrito tres cartas.* Here *haber* is a pure auxiliary — it has no meaning of its own left, the participle never changes form, and nothing can be inserted between the two words.
>
> **_Tener_ + object + participle is not a tense at all.** *Tengo escritas tres cartas.* Here *tener* keeps its lexical meaning — you genuinely possess or hold something — and the participle behaves like an **adjective describing the object**. Which is why it agrees in gender and number, and why the word order is flexible.
>
> The practical test: **can you replace the participle with an adjective and keep the structure?** *Tengo la cena preparada* / *tengo la cena lista* — both work, same shape. Try that with *he preparado la cena* and it collapses. That is the clearest evidence that *tener* here is a main verb with a predicate, not an auxiliary.

# ② El resultativo: *tener* + participio

### Prosa

> El participio **concuerda con el objeto** en género y número. Esta es la diferencia visible más inmediata frente a *haber*, donde el participio no cambia nunca.

### La concordancia

| Con *haber* — invariable | Con *tener* — concuerda |
|---|---|
| He escrito **las cartas**. | Tengo **escritas** las cartas. |
| He lavado **los platos**. | Tengo **lavados** los platos. |
| He leído **tres páginas**. | Tengo **leídas** tres páginas. |
| He preparado **la cena**. | Tengo **preparada** la cena. |

### El orden es flexible

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo **preparada la cena**. | Dinner's ready. |
| Tengo **la cena preparada**. | *(same — both orders are natural)* |

### Qué verbos admiten esta construcción

| Sí — transitivos con resultado | No |
|---|---|
| escribir, lavar, leer, preparar, terminar, guardar, ordenar, revisar, resuelto | ~~tengo ido~~ · ~~tengo sido~~ · ~~tengo tenido~~ · ~~tengo sabido~~ \* |

> Solo funciona con verbos **transitivos** que dejan un resultado visible en el objeto. Un verbo de movimiento o de estado no deja nada que sostener.

### El matiz de acumulación

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| **Tengo leídos** tres capítulos. | I've got three chapters read. — *progress toward a total.* |
| **Tengo revisadas** la mitad de las pruebas. | I've got half the tests marked. |
| **Tengo vistos** casi todos sus cuadros. | I've seen nearly all his paintings. |

### NOTA-B · ② — What the resultative actually adds

> **NOTA\*** *He escrito las cartas* and *tengo escritas las cartas* both describe the same event. The difference is where the sentence puts its weight.
>
> **_Haber_ reports the action.** The letters got written; the sentence is about the writing.
>
> **_Tener_ reports the resulting state, and attributes it to the subject.** The letters are now in a written condition, and I am the one who holds them that way. That is why the construction feels like taking stock — it is the natural verb for inventories, progress checks, and "where do things stand" contexts.
>
> **Two consequences worth noticing:**
> - It implies the result **persists**. *Tengo escritas las cartas* means they are still written and available. If they were destroyed, the sentence stops being true, which is not the case for *he escrito las cartas*.
> - It tends to want a **quantified or definite object**: *tengo escritas tres cartas*, *tengo escritas las cartas*. A bare mass noun sits badly — *tengo escrita correspondencia* is odd. \*
>
> There is often an **accumulative reading** as well: *tengo leídos tres capítulos* suggests three so far, with more to come. That flavour is stronger still with *llevar* — see the next section.

# ③ *Haber* · *tener* · *llevar* + participio

### Prosa

> Tres verbos, el mismo participio, tres significados distintos. Este contraste es la manera más rápida de fijar qué aporta cada uno.

### El contraste

| Verbo | Ejemplo | Qué aporta |
|---|---|---|
| **haber** | He leído tres capítulos. | La acción, sin más. Participio invariable. |
| **tener** | Tengo leídos tres capítulos. | El resultado, en mi poder. Concuerda. |
| **llevar** | Llevo leídos tres capítulos. | El avance dentro de un total. Concuerda. |

### Ejemplos

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| **He escrito** cincuenta páginas. | I've written fifty pages. |
| **Tengo escritas** cincuenta páginas. | I've got fifty pages written. |
| **Llevo escritas** cincuenta páginas. | I'm fifty pages in. |

### NOTA-C · ③ — *Tener* vs. *llevar*

> **NOTA\*** These two are close enough that it is worth naming the difference precisely.
>
> **_Tener_ takes stock.** It describes the current holdings, with no necessary implication about what comes next. *Tengo revisadas veinte pruebas* — twenty are done. Whether there are more is not part of the sentence.
>
> **_Llevar_ measures progress.** It presupposes a **total** and locates you inside it. *Llevo revisadas veinte pruebas* strongly implies a stack still to go. Ask *¿cuántas llevas?* and you are asking how far through something someone is.
>
> So *llevar* is the verb of the marathon, *tener* the verb of the inventory. They overlap constantly, and in many sentences either is fine — but where a total is in view, *llevar* is the more idiomatic choice.

# ④ Las formas fijas — sin concordancia

### Prosa

> Algunas combinaciones de *tener* + participio se han **lexicalizado**. Ya no llevan objeto nominal sino una cláusula o un infinitivo, y por eso el participio queda **invariable**.

### Ejemplos

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| **Tengo entendido que** renunció. | I understand he resigned. |
| **Tengo oído que** van a cerrar. | I hear they're going to close. |
| **Tengo pensado** mudarme. | I'm planning to move. |
| **Tengo previsto** llegar el jueves. | I'm scheduled to arrive Thursday. |
| **Tengo comprobado que** no sirve. | I've verified it doesn't work. \* |

### El contraste

| Con objeto — concuerda | Con cláusula — invariable |
|---|---|
| Tengo **entendidas** las instrucciones. | Tengo **entendido** que renunció. |
| Tengo **pensadas** dos opciones. | Tengo **pensado** mudarme. |

### NOTA-D · ④ — Why the frozen forms don't agree

> **NOTA\*** The rule has not changed; there is simply nothing for the participle to agree **with**.
>
> Agreement in section ② happens because the participle describes a **noun**: *las cartas* is feminine plural, so *escritas* is feminine plural. In *tengo entendido que renunció*, what follows is a **clause**, not a noun. Clauses have no gender or number, so the participle falls back to the default masculine singular and stays there.
>
> The same explanation covers *tengo pensado mudarme* — an infinitive, not a noun. And it predicts the contrast correctly: give the same phrase a real nominal object and agreement returns. *Tengo pensadas dos opciones.* Same verb, same participle, different complement.
>
> **A warning that belongs here.** *Tengo entendido* takes a bare *que* — <em>tengo entendido **de** que</em> is dequeísmo. See section ⑤b of the parent page.
>
> Note also that *tener pensado* and *tener previsto* appear on the parent page among the modals. That is the same construction seen from the other side: there as expressions of intent, here as frozen participles.

# ⑤ El reproche: *te lo tengo dicho*

### Prosa

> Con verbos de habla, *tener* + participio adquiere un valor de **insistencia**: no es que lo haya dicho una vez, es que lo vengo diciendo.

### Ejemplos

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| **Te lo tengo dicho**: no toques eso. | I've told you: don't touch that. |
| **Te tengo dicho** que llames antes de venir. | I've told you to call before coming. |
| **Ya te lo tengo advertido**. | I've warned you about this. |
| **Nos lo tienen prohibido**. | We're not allowed to. |

### NOTA-E · ⑤ — Repetition, and a warning about tone

> **NOTA\*** This is a small, high-frequency corner of the construction, and the difference from the plain perfect is entirely pragmatic.
>
> *Te he dicho que no* reports that the telling happened. **_Te lo tengo dicho_ reports that the telling is a standing fact** — said before, said more than once, and still in force. English gets close with "I've told you" said with emphasis, or "I keep telling you."
>
> **The tone is almost always reproachful.** It is what a parent says to a child, or one adult says to another when patience is running out. It is not neutral, and using it in a workplace or with someone you don't know well will land as scolding. Recognize it freely; deploy it deliberately.
>
> The agreement rule still applies underneath: in *te lo tengo dicho*, the object is the neuter *lo*, so the participle is masculine singular. Give it a plural nominal object and it agrees normally — *te tengo dichas muchas cosas*.

# ⑥ La negativa: *tener sin* + infinitivo

### Prosa

> El resultativo tiene un contrario exacto. Para decir que algo **sigue pendiente**, se usa *tener sin* más infinitivo — y aquí no hay participio, así que no hay concordancia.

### Ejemplos

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| **Tengo sin lavar** los platos. | I've still got the dishes unwashed. |
| **Tengo sin leer** tres libros. | I've got three books still unread. |
| Todavía **tenemos sin resolver** ese problema. | We've still got that problem unresolved. |

### El par

| Hecho | Pendiente |
|---|---|
| Tengo **lavados** los platos. | Tengo **sin lavar** los platos. |
| Tengo **leídos** tres libros. | Tengo **sin leer** tres libros. |

### NOTA-F · ⑥ — The mirror of the resultative

> **NOTA\*** This construction is much less taught than the affirmative resultative and just as common in speech.
>
> The logic is transparent once you see the pair: *tengo lavados los platos* holds the dishes in a washed condition; *tengo sin lavar los platos* holds them in an **unwashed** one. Both are statements about the current state of my responsibilities. The second is the natural way to describe a backlog.
>
> **The form is simpler than the affirmative:** *sin* plus a bare infinitive, invariable, no agreement to worry about. Note the asymmetry — the affirmative uses a participle, the negative uses an infinitive. There is no *tengo no lavados los platos*.
>
> Word order is flexible here too: *tengo sin leer tres libros* and *tengo tres libros sin leer* are both good, the second slightly more common. \*

# ⑦ *Tener a alguien por* — la valoración

### Prosa

> Con la preposición *por*, la construcción deja de describir un estado físico y pasa a expresar **un juicio**: cómo considero a alguien o algo.

### Fórmula

```
tener + a alguien + por + sustantivo / adjetivo
```

### Ejemplos

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| Lo **tengo por** un buen médico. | I consider him a good doctor. |
| La **tengo por** muy capaz. | I regard her as very capable. |
| Siempre lo **tuve por** honesto. | I always took him to be honest. |
| Se **tiene por** experto en la materia. | He considers himself an expert on the subject. |
| Doy eso **por** sabido. | I take that as given. *— related pattern with dar.* |

### NOTA-G · ⑦ — Register, and the *se* variant

> **NOTA\*** This is the most formal item on the page, and it is worth knowing where it sits.
>
> *Tener a alguien por algo* means **to consider, to regard as** — it reports the subject's assessment, not a fact. It belongs to careful speech and writing: a report, a character reference, a considered opinion. In casual conversation *creo que es un buen médico* or *me parece muy capaz* will be far more common.
>
> **The reflexive is where it turns up most.** *Se tiene por experto* — he considers himself an expert — and this reading is frequently ironic, implying the self-assessment is generous. That flavour is worth hearing.
>
> Two neighbours in the same family, both more literary still: *tener en mucho* and *tener en poco* — to think highly, or little, of someone. Recognition only.
